Monthly Dividend ETFs vs Individual Monthly Payers: JEPI, JEPQ and QYLD vs O, MAIN and AGNC

By DivToCrypto · Nov 22, 2026 · Monthly Dividend Stocks · 8 min read

If you want a monthly dividend, you have two basic choices. You can buy a fund that pays monthly, like JEPI or QYLD. Or you can buy individual companies that pay monthly, like Realty Income (O), Main Street Capital (MAIN) or AGNC Investment (AGNC).

Both show up near the top of our Top 30 monthly dividend stocks list, and both can put cash in your account every month. But the cash comes from very different places, and the risks are different too. This post compares them side by side with current facts from the funds' and companies' own documents.

Educational, not financial advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security.

The short version

  • Covered-call income ETFs (JEPI, JEPQ, QYLD, DIVO) own a basket of stocks and sell call options for extra income. You get diversification and a high, variable payout, but you give up some upside and pay a yearly fee.
  • Individual monthly payers (O, MAIN, AGNC) pay from one business's rent, loan interest or mortgage spread. There's no fund fee, and some have long records of raising their payout, but all your risk sits with one company.

The funds: what they are

JEPI (JPMorgan Equity Premium Income ETF). An actively managed portfolio of U.S. large-cap stocks plus equity-linked notes (ELNs) that sell call options on the S&P 500. It can hold up to 20% of assets in ELNs and aims to pay monthly distributions at a relatively stable level (summary prospectus). Launched May 20, 2020, with $46.16 billion in assets at October 31, 2026 (fact sheet).

JEPQ (JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF). The same approach, built around the Nasdaq-100 (summary prospectus). Launched May 3, 2022, with $42.49 billion in assets at October 31, 2026 (fact sheet).

QYLD (Global X Nasdaq 100 Covered Call ETF). Owns the Nasdaq-100 stocks and sells index call options against them, a classic "buy-write." Launched December 11, 2013, with $7.33 billion in net assets as of November 20, 2026 (Global X).

DIVO (Amplify CWP Enhanced Dividend Income ETF). A concentrated portfolio of about 30 dividend stocks with selective covered calls. It targets roughly 2% to 3% a year from dividends and 2% to 4% from option premiums (Amplify). Launched December 14, 2016.

The individual payers: what they are

Realty Income (O) owns thousands of buildings leased to tenants like stores and warehouses. In November 2026 it raised its monthly dividend to $0.2740, which it calls its 137th increase since listing in 1994 (press release). By our math on the company's guidance, the dividend is about 74% of expected 2026 AFFO. Our full breakdown: Realty Income (O), Explained.

Main Street Capital (MAIN) lends to and invests in lower-middle-market companies. It pays a regular monthly dividend of $0.265, rising to $0.27 for January through March 2027, plus quarterly supplementals that have recently been $0.30. The company says it has never reduced its regular monthly dividend since its 2007 IPO (announcement). Our breakdown: Main Street Capital (MAIN), Explained.

AGNC Investment (AGNC) borrows to buy government-agency mortgage bonds and earns the spread. It declared $0.12 for November 2026 (AGNC). Its tangible net book value fell 16.9% in Q3 2026 to $7.13 per share, with a -12.7% economic return for the quarter and 7.9x "at risk" leverage (Q3 results). AGNC is our only monthly dividend holding right now.

Where the money really comes from

This is the most important difference.

  • Covered-call ETFs pay from stock dividends plus option premiums. Selling a call brings in cash now, but it caps how much you gain if the market rallies hard. JPMorgan's prospectus says this directly: selling calls "limits its opportunity to profit from an increase in the market value." The payout rises in choppy, volatile markets and can shrink when markets are calm.
  • Equity REITs like O pay from rent. Rent tends to rise slowly with leases, which is why O has a long record of small raises.
  • BDCs like MAIN pay from interest on loans. If rates fall or borrowers struggle, income can drop.
  • Mortgage REITs like AGNC pay from the spread between borrowing costs and mortgage-bond yields, using leverage. That's how the yield gets so high, and why book value can swing sharply.

The trade-offs

Diversification. JEPI held 140 stocks at September 30, 2026 (Invest & Compound, from issuer data), and QYLD holds 102 (Global X). One bad company barely matters. With an individual payer, one bad year for that company hits your income directly.

Upside. Covered-call funds give up part of a strong rally. Our list notes that QYLD's "payout and price erode over time," a known pattern for buy-write funds that pay out most of their option income. Individual payers can grow their dividend and price over time, as O has done, but they can also cut.

Predictability. JPMorgan aims for "relatively stable" monthly payouts, but the amounts still vary. O and MAIN set a fixed monthly amount in advance, which makes budgeting easier.

Fees. At $10,000, the difference between JEPI (0.35%) and QYLD (0.60%) is $25 a year. That's small, but it's paid every year whether the fund does well or not.

Taxes. Much of a covered-call fund's distribution is often taxed as ordinary income. JPMorgan's prospectus notes distributions may be taxed as ordinary income or capital gains. Many REIT and BDC dividends also aren't "qualified" dividends (IRS Publication 550). In a taxable account, ask a tax professional.

How we think about it

Our plan uses monthly payouts as fuel for a trading book. For that, we care most about the check showing up. We use a simple split from our earlier posts: Core names with long records and moderate yields (O, MAIN, ADC), and Boosters with high yields and more risk (AGNC today). A fund like JEPI could sit in the Core bucket for diversification. A high-yield fund like QYLD behaves more like a Booster because of payout and price erosion. We haven't bought any of the ETFs yet. FAQ

Is JEPI safer than Realty Income? They carry different risks. JEPI spreads risk across about 140 stocks but gives up some upside and has a variable payout. O depends on one company's tenants, but its payout has a long record of raises.

Why is AGNC's yield so much higher than O's? Because the market prices in more risk. AGNC uses leverage, its book value moves with interest rates, and it has cut its dividend before. A higher yield usually means higher risk.

Do covered-call ETFs lose value over time? Some do. Funds that sell calls on the whole portfolio and pay out most of the income, like QYLD, have tended to lag in strong markets. Check a fund's total return, not just its yield.

Can I own both? Yes. Many income investors pair a diversified income fund with a few individual payers. Just count the overlap and the total risk.

How do I compare yields fairly? Use the same method. Our list uses the latest monthly payout × 12 ÷ price. For funds, also check the 30-day SEC yield on the fund's site.
