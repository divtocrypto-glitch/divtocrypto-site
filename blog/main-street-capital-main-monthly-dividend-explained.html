Main Street Capital (MAIN): Monthly Dividends Plus Supplementals, Explained

By DivToCrypto · Nov 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Main Street Capital (ticker MAIN) is number two on our Top 30 monthly dividend stocks list, right behind Realty Income, and it's one of the core names in our 20 monthly dividend stocks plan. Unlike Realty Income (O), a landlord, MAIN earns its money by lending to and investing in smaller private companies.

Educational only, not financial advice. We don't own MAIN yet, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

What Main Street Capital is

Main Street is a business development company, or BDC. A BDC is a public company that lends money to, and often takes ownership stakes in, private businesses. Like a REIT, it must pass most taxable income to shareholders, so yields run high.

According to the company's own description, it focuses on two groups:

  • Lower middle market (LMM) companies, generally with annual revenue between $10 million and $150 million.
  • Private loan companies, generally with revenue between $25 million and $500 million, often owned by private equity funds.

Its Q3 2026 results show that as of September 30, 2026 it held investments in 92 LMM companies (about $3.25 billion at fair value) and 86 private loan companies (about $2.11 billion). About 99% of its debt investments at cost were secured by a first-priority lien, meaning first in line if a borrower fails.

The dividend: monthly plus supplemental

This is what makes MAIN interesting for us. It pays two kinds of dividends:

  1. A regular monthly dividend. For October, November and December 2026 it's $0.265 per share each month, per the Q4 2026 dividend announcement. The company says it has never reduced its regular monthly dividend since its 2007 IPO.
  2. A quarterly supplemental dividend. Recently it has been $0.30 per share each quarter, paid out of extra taxable income. The company calls the December 2026 payment its twenty-first consecutive quarterly supplemental. They aren't promised.

On November 3, 2026, Main Street announced a raise to $0.27 per share a month for January through March 2027, a 1.9% increase, plus another $0.30 supplemental payable in December.

Yield. The company put its annualized current yield at 7.9% based on the November 2, 2026 close of $56.30. StockAnalysis shows a $4.38 annual dividend and a 7.80% yield. That number includes supplementals. Counting only the regular $0.265 monthly dividend, our own math at the Nov 16 close of $56.18 gives about 5.7%. The gap matters: the supplementals could shrink in a bad year.

Is the dividend covered?

For BDCs the key number is net investment income (NII): interest and dividends earned minus expenses. In Q3 2026, Main Street reported NII of $0.89 per share and distributable NII of $0.96 per share (source). Regular monthly dividends for the quarter were $0.795, so they were covered. Total dividends paid in Q3, including the supplemental, were $1.095, which is more than that quarter's NII. (Our comparison of the company's numbers.) The supplemental depends on extra income and gains.

The risks

  • Price above book value. Net asset value (NAV) was $34.22 per share on September 30. At about $56, MAIN trades roughly 64% above that by our math. If sentiment turns, that premium can shrink fast, even if the dividend holds.
  • Credit risk. It lends to small, private companies. As of September 30, investments on non-accrual (borrowers not paying as agreed) were 0.9% of the portfolio at fair value and 3.5% at cost. A recession could push that higher.
  • Falling rates. Many of its loans are floating-rate. The company said lower benchmark rates already weighed on interest income in Q3.
  • Supplementals can shrink. About a quarter of the 7.8% headline yield comes from supplementals that aren't guaranteed.
  • Taxes. BDC dividends are mostly taxed as ordinary income, not at the lower qualified dividend rate.
