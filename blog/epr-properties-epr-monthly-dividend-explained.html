EPR Properties (EPR): A 6% Monthly Dividend Built on Movie Theaters, Topgolf and Ski Hills

By DivToCrypto · Nov 27, 2026 · Monthly Dividend Stocks · 9 min read

EPR Properties (ticker EPR) is the monthly dividend stock on our list with the most interesting comeback story. In 2020 it stopped paying its dividend completely. Today it pays $0.31 a share every month, and it has raised that payout four times since restarting. But the current rate is still below where it was before the pandemic, and about a third of its rent still comes from movie theaters.

So EPR is a good test of a question every monthly dividend investor faces: how much should a past dividend suspension count against a company that has since recovered?

Educational only, not financial advice. DivToCrypto does not own EPR. According to our public Trades page and dashboard data, our only monthly dividend holding right now is AGNC.

What EPR owns: "experiential" real estate

EPR is a real estate investment trust (REIT) based in Kansas City. It owns or finances properties where people go to do something: watch a movie, swing a golf club, ride a roller coaster, ski or work out. The company calls this experiential real estate: you can't stream a ski run or a Topgolf outing.

According to its Q3 2026 results, EPR had $7.6 billion of total investments on September 30, 2026. Experiential properties made up 95% and education properties (mostly private schools) the other 5%. The wholly owned portfolio was 99% leased or operated.

Here's how its rent splits up, from the Q3 2026 supplemental report (page 19). ABR means annualized base revenue: yearly rent plus mortgage interest.

The "Reduce" label on theatres is the key detail. Management is putting new money into fitness, eat-and-play and attractions instead. In Q3 it bought two eat-and-play and two fitness properties and began funding a Live Nation concert venue in Portland, Oregon.

How EPR makes money

Most of EPR's leases are triple-net. The tenant pays rent plus the property's taxes, insurance and upkeep. EPR also makes mortgage loans secured by experiential properties and collects the interest. In Q3 2026, EPR reported $204.1 million of total revenue, of which $175.3 million was rent and $14.6 million was mortgage and financing income.

The top ten customers produced 68.0% of ABR. Topgolf is first at 14.5%. The next three are theater chains: AMC at 14.2%, Regal at 10.2% and Cinemark at 6.3%. Together, those three cinema operators make up 30.7% of ABR. That's the biggest single risk in this stock.

The dividend: amount, schedule and recent dates

EPR pays monthly. The current rate is $0.31 per share, or $3.72 a year. The company says that's a 5.1% increase over the annual rate at the end of 2025. At the $60.26 quote on the morning of November 27, 2026 (StockAnalysis), that works out to a yield of about 6.2%. StockAnalysis lists 6.18%.

When COVID-19 closed theaters and parks in 2020, tenants couldn't pay rent, and EPR suspended the common dividend as part of getting covenant relief from its lenders. The next payment came about 15 months later. Management restarted at "a prudent level which should allow us to grow the dividends over time," and it has. But by our math, today's $3.72 is still about 19% below the $4.59 rate from early 2020. Anyone who bought for income in 2019 has never been made whole on the payout.

Is the dividend covered today?

StockAnalysis shows a scary-looking 104.77% payout ratio, but that's based on GAAP earnings, which subtract large non-cash depreciation. REITs are better judged on AFFO (adjusted funds from operations). On that basis, coverage looks healthy:

  • Q3 2026 AFFO was $1.54 per share, up 10.8% from a year earlier. Dividends declared in the quarter were $0.93. EPR's own reported AFFO payout ratio was 60%, down from 64% a year earlier.
  • 2026 guidance for FFO as adjusted is $5.49 to $5.57 per share, raised in October. Against the $3.72 annual rate, that's a payout of roughly 67% to 68% by our math.
  • Debt: net debt was 5.1x annualized adjusted EBITDAre and interest coverage was 4.0x. As of September 30, EPR had nothing drawn on its $1.0 billion revolving credit line.

Keeping about a third of cash flow after the dividend leaves room to absorb a bad tenant. The 2020 suspension wasn't caused by a high payout ratio; revenue stopped almost overnight. That's the real risk.

What $1,000 would pay each month

Using the $60.26 quote and the $0.31 monthly dividend:

  • $1,000 buys about 16.59 shares.
  • That pays about $5.14 a month, or $61.73 a year.

With dividends reinvested at the same price and rate for 12 months, the share count would grow to about 17.65, paying about $5.47 a month. Real results will differ: the price moves, and the board can change the dividend.

Risks to know before buying

  1. Theater exposure. Theaters are 34% of ABR; a weak box office or a struggling chain hits rent directly.
  2. Tenant concentration. The top ten customers are 68% of ABR, so one tenant in trouble matters a lot.
  3. Consumer spending. People cut golf outings, ski trips and theme parks before groceries.
  4. Share issuance. In Q3, EPR agreed to forward sales of about 1.1 million new shares at an average $60.79, which spreads earnings across more shares.
  5. Taxes. REIT dividends are mostly taxed as ordinary income in a taxable account. See IRS Publication 550.

FAQ

Does EPR pay dividends monthly? Yes. EPR pays $0.31 per share every month. The next ex-dividend date is November 30, 2026, with payment on December 15, 2026.

Did EPR ever cut its dividend? Yes, in the most severe way. It suspended the common dividend after the May 15, 2020 payment and resumed in July 2021 at $0.25 a month, below the old $0.3825 rate. It has raised it four times since, to $0.31.

Is EPR's dividend safe now? No dividend is guaranteed. In Q3 2026, EPR's reported AFFO payout ratio was 60%, and it raised its 2026 earnings guidance. That's healthy coverage, but a big drop in consumer spending or trouble at a large theater tenant could change it.

How much would $1,000 in EPR pay? At the $60.26 quote, about 16.59 shares, paying roughly $5.14 a month or $61.73 a year at the current $0.31 rate.
