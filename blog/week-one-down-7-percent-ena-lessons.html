Week One: Down 7% and What ENA Taught Me

By Keith · Oct 31, 2026 · 4 min read

The first week of DivToCrypto is in the books, and I'm down. Not a disaster, but enough to sting and enough to learn from. Here's every trade, the real numbers, and the lessons I'm taking into week two. Everything below is also on the live dashboard if you want to check my math.

My first-week P&L

These numbers are from the dashboard update on October 31, 2026, using Robinhood mark prices as of 7:00 AM CT that morning.

  • Net invested: $179.47 ($204.80 of buys, minus $25.33 from one sale)
  • Current value: $166.30
  • Realized P&L: −$4.57, all from ENA, which I fully sold
  • Unrealized P&L: −$8.60 on my XCN position
  • Total P&L: −$13.17, or about −7.3%
  • Dividends received: $0. I don't own any dividend stocks yet.

What happened with XCN

XCN is my main crypto position, and right now it's my only one. I bought it in pieces. On October 25, my log note says I'd deployed half of a deposit and kept the rest for a dip. On October 29, I added $35.02 more at a lower price than any of my earlier buys.

At the latest mark price, the position is worth $166.30 against a cost basis of $174.90. That's the −$8.60 unrealized loss, a little under 5%. For a coin like XCN, a swing that size isn't unusual. It's not a realized loss unless I sell, but I'm not going to pretend it isn't there.

What happened with ENA

This is the trade I actually learned from.

On October 25, I bought $29.90 of ENA as a short-term trade. My log note at the time reads: target about +4%, stop about −5%, profits to XCN. Small, defined, with a plan.

On October 29, I sold all 143.88 ENA for $25.33. That's a realized loss of $4.57, or about −15.3%, roughly three times the stop I'd written down. The same day, I put $35.02 into XCN.

I want to be precise here: the −5% stop was in my notes. The exit happened at −15%. Whatever the mechanics, the plan said one thing and the result said another, and that gap is the lesson.

One more twist: at the October 31 dashboard update, ENA's mark price was above where I sold. That doesn't prove holding would have been right, and I'm not chasing it. It's just a reminder that a late exit can end up badly timed in both directions.

Lesson 1: A stop has to be a real order

A number in a notebook doesn't protect you. If I decide a trade gets cut at −5%, that exit needs to exist at the broker the moment I buy, not as an intention I'll act on later while I'm watching the chart, or not watching it.

From here on, any short-term trade with a stop gets an actual stop order entered when I buy, or I don't take the trade.

It's worth knowing that even a real stop isn't a guarantee. In a fast drop, a stop can fill below the price you set. That's one more reason to keep these trades small.

Lesson 2: Position sizing matters more than picks

When I bought ENA, it was $29.90 out of about $170 I'd put into crypto at that point. If the −5% stop had held, the loss would have been about a dollar and a half. Instead it was $4.57, my entire realized loss for the week.

The bigger sizing issue is actually XCN. Every dollar I have in crypto right now is in one coin. That's concentrated by any definition, and it's why the whole dashboard moves with a single asset. As dividends start coming in, I want to be more deliberate about how big any one position is allowed to get.

My working rules going forward:

  • Short-term trades stay small and get a real stop order at entry.
  • Decide the exit before the entry, both the target and the stop, and write it in the log.
  • No single trade should be able to define the week. If one trade can, it was too big.

Lesson 3: The boring part hasn't started yet

The project is called Dividends to Crypto, and week one had zero dividends. Everything so far is crypto bought with cash. That's the next thing to fix.

The plan is to start a monthly dividend core with five positions, O, MAIN, JEPI, PECO and BND, at about $20 each, and let those payments fund future crypto buys. I explain the full list in The 20 Monthly Dividend Stocks I'm Building Around.

The plan for week two

  • Buy the starter five dividend payers.
  • No new short-term crypto trades unless the stop is a real order.
  • Keep logging every trade and keep the dashboard current.
  • Post the week two recap here with the same numbers, good or bad.

Publishing a 7% loss in week one isn't fun. But a public log only means something if it includes weeks like this one. If you're starting something similar, here's the cheapest lesson I can pass along: write the exit down, then make it real at the broker before you need it.
