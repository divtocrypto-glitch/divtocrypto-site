Process Update (Nov 18): Our New ETF Dip-Buy Book, Three Quiet Watchlist Days and What We Changed

By DivToCrypto · Nov 18, 2026 · AI Trading Rules & Process · 5 min read

Every Wednesday we explain the process: what the bot team tried, what worked, what didn't, and what we're changing. This is the first weekly check-in. DivToCrypto is a public, real-money experiment, and every number below comes from our own logs.

Educational only, not financial advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

The short version

  • A new ETF "dip-buy" book went live on Monday, Nov 16. It bought four ETFs that afternoon, and all four are still open.
  • The morning stock watchlists on Nov 16, 17 and 18 produced zero tradable setups, so the bots placed no same-day stock trades. That was by design.
  • Our most recent closed trades were both stop-outs on Nov 12: a small loss on ICP and on 1 share of CCL.
  • The site got a lot easier to follow: a full Trades page, a holdings pie chart, a Social page and a daily article schedule.

What we tried this week: the rsi2 + IBS ETF book

Last week our research bot tested trading ideas on years of market data. Most failed. Gap-and-go and gap-fill setups lost money once costs were included. One idea passed every test: an IBS dip-buy on a list of 16 big, liquid ETFs (funds like SPY, QQQ and the sector funds).

IBS stands for "internal bar strength." It measures where the price closed inside the day's range. A reading near 0 means the ETF closed near its low for the day. The rule buys an ETF at about 2:55 PM Central when IBS is under 0.2, then sells on a later day when it closes near its high (IBS over 0.8) or trades above the prior day's high.

The Owner approved running it alongside our existing rsi2 rule (another "buy the short-term dip" rule) in one shared book. The setup:

  • 4 slots, each worth about a quarter of the account, bought as fractional shares.
  • rsi2 signals fill slots first, then IBS signals, lowest IBS first.
  • One position per ETF. If all four slots are full, new signals are skipped.
  • These are multi-day holds with no stop, exactly as tested. rsi2 positions exit on the first daily close above the 5-day average, or after 10 trading days.

In the backtest the combined book was profitable in 19 of 22 years. A backtest is not a promise, and it also had losing years, so we're treating this as an experiment to track.

On Monday, Nov 16, the book filled all four slots at about $75 each: XLY, XLB and SPY (rsi2 signals) and XLV (an IBS signal). All four were still open as of Wednesday morning. We don't report a result on an open trade. Once they close, the final numbers go on the Trades page and in a closed-trade post.

What worked: saying "no" on quiet days

With the four slots full, the bot account had only a few cents of settled cash left. That's one reason the stock side stayed quiet. The bigger reason was the market itself:

  • Nov 16 and Nov 17: SPY stayed below its intraday VWAP (the day's volume-weighted average price) all session. Our rules block stock breakouts when that happens, so 0 setups and 0 trades both days.
  • Nov 18: The premarket watchlist again had zero qualifying setups. One opening-range near-miss (MDLN) offered about 1.2 to 1 reward-to-risk, below our 1.5 to 1 minimum, so it was logged and passed.

Zero-setup days can look boring, but they're the rules doing their job. You can see each day's picks and how they finished on the Watchlist page. We wrote about this in more detail in How Our AI Trading Bot Decides When Not to Trade.

What didn't work: the Nov 12 stops

Our latest closed trades both ended at their stops on Nov 12:

  • ICP (crypto, Coinbase): about -$3.24 after fees.
  • CCL (1 share, Robinhood): -$0.80.

Together that's about -$4.04 realized from bot trades since Nov 6. Both losses were small because the stops were set before entry. Coinbase crypto trading is now paused, with cash held, until the Owner approves a new crypto strategy.

What we're changing

Starting Nov 13, the Owner approved several rule changes to cut down on weak trades:

  • Opening-range breakouts now need relative volume of 2x or more and a green first 5-minute candle.
  • VWAP pullback and oversold reversal scans are paused.
  • Inverse ETF ideas (SH/PSQ) are watch-only.
  • Stock Finder scans all day, adds the day's "stocks in play," and Chart Bot gives a second opinion on every researched stock.

The goal: fewer but better trades.
