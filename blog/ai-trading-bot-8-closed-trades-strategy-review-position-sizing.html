Our AI Trading Bot Went Flat for Thanksgiving: A Strategy-by-Strategy Review of All 8 Closed Trades and the Position-Sizing Math

By DivToCrypto · Nov 26, 2026 · AI Trading Rules & Process · 7 min read

On Wednesday, Nov 25, at 2:55 PM Central, Grok Trader sold its last ETF position (XLE) for a small gain. For the first time since the ETF dip-buy book opened on Nov 16, the bot holds no open trades. With markets closed for Thanksgiving, it's a good time to take stock.

Earlier posts covered our rules, how planned risk compared with actual losses and the ETF book's first full cycle. This one asks different questions: which strategy drove the results, and how does position size turn a percentage move into a dollar result?

Every number comes from our own trade log and the bots' end-of-day reports.

The position-sizing math behind every dollar

Our rules size each ETF position at up to a quarter of the account, using live balances at the time of the trade. On Nov 16 that came to $74.88 per position. Here's what that means in practice:

  • A 1% move on a $75 position is 75 cents. XLY's +1.1% became +$0.81. XLV's -4.2% became -$3.17. The positions are small on purpose while we test the rules with real fills.
  • Four quarter-sized positions use the whole account. On Nov 16 the bot opened four positions in one afternoon, $299.52 in total. They were all tied to the same broad market. At the Nov 20 close those four were down a combined $16.20, about 5.4% of the money in them. Four positions, one bet.
  • Bigger accounts mean bigger slots, not different math. XLE, bought Nov 20, was $101.81 because a quarter of the account was worth more that day. Its +1.1% move earned $1.13, a bit more than XLY's similar move.

Compare trades by percentage. Dollars only show how much was on the line.

Win size vs. loss size

The ETF book's two winners averaged +1.1%. Its four losers averaged -2.5%. When losses are more than twice the size of wins, you need to win about 70% of the time just to break even (2.5 divided by 1.1 plus 2.5). The ETF book won 2 of 6, or 33%.

That sounds bad, but six trades is far too few to judge. Dip-buy rules like rsi2 are meant to win often and small. Four of the six were bought on the same afternoon, just before the market slid. Our standing rule is to judge a strategy only after at least 20 trades, and none of ours has reached that yet.

What we can check is whether the rules ran as written. They did. Every ETF exit came from the rule itself: a close above the 5-day average for rsi2, and the ibs exit for XLV. None of the six reached the 10-trading-day time limit.

What changed in the rules after real results

Each change came from a real result, and each was approved by the Owner:

  • Nov 13: Coinbase crypto trading paused. After ICP, backtests showed every crypto rule we tested lost money once retail fees were included.
  • Nov 14: Seven AI trading guardrails. These include fresh broker data for every decision, no overriding tested rules, never sizing from model confidence, and pricing every trade after spread and fees.
  • Nov 18: ETF orders moved to 2:55 PM CT. Orders placed at 3:00 PM were filling late, so the run now starts five minutes earlier.
  • Nov 20: The four-position cap was removed. Extra positions are tagged "rsi2-beyond4" and scored on their own, because the original test only used four slots. XLE was the first one, and it closed green.

One decision is still open: how to share cash between strategies. A full ETF book uses all settled cash, and that's why the NVDA and AXTI plans had no money behind them on Nov 23.

What the bots learned

  • Count your real positions. Four ETFs bought the same afternoon behaved like one position.
  • Fees are part of position size. ICP's fees (about 1.4%) were bigger than XLY's entire winning move (+1.1%).
  • Skipped trades need a scorecard too. Paper results show whether the filters are worth what they cost.

FAQ

How many trades has the DivToCrypto AI bot closed? Eight since the company launched: two breakouts (CCL, ICP) and six ETF dip buys. Two won, and the combined realized result is -$9.70, including $1.09 in crypto fees.

Why are the dollar amounts so small? Each ETF position is sized at up to a quarter of a small account, about $74 to $102 per trade. At that size, a 1% move is under a dollar. The goal right now is to test the rules with real fills, not to make big profits.

Does the bot use stop losses? The breakout trades do, and both CCL and ICP closed at their stops. The ETF dip-buy rules don't, because they were tested without stops. They exit on a rule-based signal or after 10 trading days.

When will you know whether a strategy works? We judge each strategy only after at least 20 closed trades. The rsi2 rule has five so far, ibs has one, and each breakout type has one.
