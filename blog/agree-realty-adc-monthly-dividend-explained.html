Agree Realty (ADC): Monthly Dividends From a Retail Landlord, Explained

By DivToCrypto · Nov 20, 2026 · Monthly Dividend Stocks · 4 min read

Agree Realty (ticker ADC) is number four on our Top 30 monthly dividend stocks list. Like Realty Income (O), it's a landlord that pays its dividend every month. Unlike Main Street Capital (MAIN), which lends to small private companies, ADC owns buildings rented to some of the biggest retailers in the country.

Educational only, not financial advice. We don't own ADC, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

What Agree Realty is

Agree Realty is a real estate investment trust (REIT) that focuses on retail net lease properties. "Net lease" means the tenant usually pays most of the property costs, like taxes, insurance and upkeep, on top of rent. The landlord collects a steadier, simpler stream of rent.

According to its Q3 2026 results, as of September 30, 2026 the company owned:

  • 2,888 properties in all 50 states and Washington, D.C., about 61.1 million square feet.
  • A portfolio that was about 99.8% leased, with an average of about 7.8 years left on its leases.
  • About 65.1% of rent from investment-grade tenants, meaning companies the credit agencies rate as strong borrowers.

It also has a ground lease business: 270 leases where ADC owns the land and the tenant owns the building on it. Those made up 10.0% of rent and were fully occupied. If a ground-lease tenant leaves, the land (and often the building) reverts to the landlord, which is why many investors see them as lower risk.

The dividend

ADC pays a monthly dividend. On November 10, 2026, it declared $0.271 per share, payable December 14 to holders of record on November 30. That annualizes to $3.252 per share, which the company says is 3.4% above its annualized rate from the fourth quarter of 2025.

Yield. StockAnalysis lists a 4.63% yield based on the Nov 19, 2026 close of $70.19. That's in the same neighborhood as Realty Income and far below the high-yield names on our list.

ADC raises its payout in small steps a few times a year. From the company's dividend history:

Is the dividend covered?

For REITs, the number to watch is AFFO (adjusted funds from operations), roughly the cash left over from rent after recurring costs. In Q3 2026, ADC reported AFFO of $1.15 per share, up 4.9% from a year earlier. Dividends declared for the quarter were $0.801 per share. By our math that's a payout of about 70% of AFFO, which leaves a cushion.

You may see a much higher "payout ratio" on data sites (StockAnalysis shows about 168%). That one compares the dividend to GAAP net income, which is reduced by depreciation on the buildings. For REITs, AFFO is the more useful comparison.

The risks

  • Rates. REIT prices often fall when interest rates rise, because safer bonds start paying more and borrowing costs go up. ADC issued $400 million of 10-year notes at 5.65% in Q3.
  • Lower yield. At about 4.6%, ADC generates less cash per dollar invested than many of our Top 30 picks. It's built for steadiness, not size.
  • Share issuance. ADC grows by selling new shares. In Q3 it settled 5.7 million shares of forward equity for about $426 million. That funds new properties, but it also spreads the income over more shares.
  • Retail exposure. Every tenant is a retailer. A tough stretch for stores, or a big tenant failing, would hit rent.
  • Taxes. Like most REIT dividends, ADC's are generally taxed as ordinary income, not at the lower qualified rate.
