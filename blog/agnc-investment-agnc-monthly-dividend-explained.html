AGNC Investment (AGNC): An 18% Monthly Dividend, and Why the Yield Isn't the Whole Story

By DivToCrypto · Nov 24, 2026 · Monthly Dividend Stocks · 8 min read

AGNC Investment (ticker AGNC) is the first stock DivToCrypto ever bought. That purchase on November 2, 2026 is where our company books start, and AGNC is still our only monthly dividend holding. So this deep dive is a little different from our looks at Realty Income (O), Main Street Capital (MAIN) and Agree Realty (ADC). Those companies own buildings or lend to businesses. AGNC owns no buildings and makes no loans of its own. It's closer to a leveraged bond fund, and that changes how you should read its big yield.

Educational only, not financial advice. We own a small AGNC position, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

What AGNC actually owns

AGNC is a mortgage REIT (mREIT). It buys agency mortgage-backed securities (agency MBS): bundles of home loans whose payments are guaranteed against credit losses by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or Ginnie Mae. In its Q3 2026 results, AGNC reported a $94.4 billion investment portfolio as of September 30, 2026. That included $91.4 billion of agency MBS, $2.4 billion of forward purchases in the "TBA" market and $0.6 billion of credit-risk and non-agency securities.

The guarantee matters. If a homeowner stops paying, AGNC still gets its money back. So the main risk here isn't defaults. It's interest rates and the price of the bonds themselves.

How AGNC makes money: the spread, times leverage

The business is simple in idea:

  1. Buy mortgage bonds that pay a certain yield.
  2. Borrow short-term money, mostly through repurchase agreements (repo), to buy more of them.
  3. Keep the difference, called the spread.

In Q3 2026, AGNC's average asset yield was 4.92% (excluding a one-time "catch-up" adjustment). Its combined cost of funds, including interest rate swaps, was 3.09%. After a few more adjustments, the company reported an annualized net interest spread of 1.86%, down from 2.00% the quarter before.

A 1.86% spread doesn't sound like enough to pay an 18% dividend, and on its own it isn't. The trick is leverage. AGNC reported "at risk" leverage of 7.9x tangible book value at quarter end. Roughly, every $1 of shareholder equity supports about $7.90 of bonds. A thin spread multiplied about eight times turns into a large stream of income.

Leverage works in both directions, though. When mortgage bond prices fall, an eight-times-levered portfolio loses value about eight times as fast as the equity underneath it.

Hedges: why AGNC isn't a pure bet on rates

AGNC tries to soften rate swings with hedges. As of September 30, 2026, its hedges covered 90% of its funding liabilities (97% excluding option-based hedges). The biggest piece was $74.6 billion of pay-fixed interest rate swaps at an average fixed rate of 2.96%. It also had an $11.1 billion net short Treasury position and $6.3 billion of receiver swaptions.

Hedges protect against rates rising, but they don't fix everything. The risk they can't fully cover is spread widening: mortgage bonds getting cheaper compared with Treasuries. That's what happened in Q3 2026. The CEO said that instability in the Treasury market caused "pronounced weakness" in agency MBS, which was the worst-performing part of the Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Bond Index that quarter.

Book value: the number to watch

For a mortgage REIT, tangible net book value per share (TNBV) is like the net asset value of a fund. It's what the bonds are worth after subtracting the debt, preferred stock and goodwill. Here's the trend from AGNC's own results table:

In Q3 alone, book value fell $1.45 a share (16.9%). Add back the $0.36 of dividends and AGNC's economic return for the quarter was -12.7%. Put simply, shareholders got paid $0.36 and lost $1.45 of underlying value.

Why a high yield doesn't mean a high total return

This is the most important idea in this post. Over the full year in that table, AGNC paid $1.44 in dividends while book value went from $8.28 to $7.13, a drop of $1.15. By our math, that's an economic return of about 3.5% for the year ($1.44 minus $1.15, divided by $8.28). The yield was around 18%. Most of the "income" was offset by a shrinking asset base.

The stock price is a separate number. At the Nov 23, 2026 close of $7.95 (StockAnalysis), AGNC traded about 11.5% above its last reported book value of $7.13. Book value moves daily, so the September figure may be out of date. But if you buy above book, you're paying a premium for the same bonds.

The dividend: schedule, recent dates and history

AGNC pays monthly. The current rate is $0.12 per share, or $1.44 a year. StockAnalysis lists an 18.11% yield at the $7.95 close. Recent dates from AGNC's dividend history:

Before the $0.12 era, the history looks different. AGNC switched from quarterly to monthly payments in October 2014 at $0.22 a month. Then it cut to $0.20 (May 2015), $0.18 (August 2016), $0.16 (May 2019) and $0.12 (April 2020). That's four cuts in about five years, and the monthly payout fell by almost half. When the company announced the 2020 cut, it tied it to the drop in book value during the COVID-19 market crash. The lesson: AGNC's dividend tends to follow its book value and earning power, not the other way around.

Cut risk today. In Q3 2026, AGNC earned $0.37 per share of net spread and dollar roll income (its own non-GAAP earnings measure) against $0.36 of dividends. That covers the payout, but barely. One positive: AGNC sold 97.6 million new shares in Q3 for $992 million. Selling shares above book value can add a little to book value per share for existing holders.

What $1,000 would pay, and the DRIP math

Using the $7.95 close and the $0.12 monthly dividend:

  • $1,000 buys about 125.8 shares.
  • That pays about $15.09 a month, or $181.13 a year.

With a DRIP (dividend reinvestment plan), each $0.12 buys about 1.5% more shares every month. If the price stayed at $7.95 and the dividend stayed at $0.12 for 12 months, the share count would grow to about 150.6, paying about $18.07 a month.

That "if" is doing a lot of work. In the past year, book value fell about 14% ($8.28 to $7.13). If the price follows book value down, reinvested dividends buy more shares at lower prices, but the account balance can still shrink. A DRIP compounds the share count. It doesn't guarantee the dollars grow.

FAQ

Is AGNC's dividend safe? No dividend is guaranteed. In Q3 2026, AGNC's $0.37 of net spread income covered its $0.36 dividend, but only just. The company has cut its monthly rate four times since 2015.

Why is AGNC's yield so high? Leverage. AGNC borrows about $7.90 for every $1 of equity to buy government-guaranteed mortgage bonds, which multiplies a spread of under 2%. It also means more risk to book value.

When is the next AGNC dividend? The next ex-dividend date is November 30, 2026, with $0.12 per share paid on December 9, 2026.

Are AGNC dividends qualified? Generally not. Like most REIT dividends, they're usually taxed as ordinary income in a taxable account. See IRS Publication 550 or a tax professional.
